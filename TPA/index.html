TPA — mechanical firmness#

This section covers texture testing of the pure oleogel, before it goes into any recipe. (For texture testing of the finished sausage, see TPA/SAUSAGES.)

Protocol. Oleogel (2%, 4%, 6%, 8% RBX) and lard were set directly in a plastic pot and, after 24h, penetrated with a 20mm probe at 1mm/s to a depth of 15mm (12 replicates each). Because the sample stuck to the probe on withdrawal, only a single compression could be run — not the usual two-cycle TPA test — so hardness (durability under a second bite) isn’t measured here. What is measured is:

  • F0 — the force at the first significant peak, i.e. the surface breaking under the probe.

  • F1 — the peak force reached during the compression.

  • Backward extrusion — softer, more fluid samples get pushed sideways rather than resisting the probe, which shows up as the force dropping after F0 before recovering. This makes F1 hard to compare directly across samples with very different backward-extrusion behavior.

What this tells us#

The 2% oleogel is dramatically softer than everything else — its F0 is roughly a fifth of the others’ — while 4%, 6%, 8% RBX and lard all show broadly comparable resistance to breaking the surface (F0). Backward extrusion becomes less pronounced as RBX% increases, meaning the gel behaves more like a cohesive solid and less like a fluid being pushed aside as wax content rises. Overall, firmness gains from adding more wax level off well before 8% — the biggest jump is between 2% and 4% RBX.
