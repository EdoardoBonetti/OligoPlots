# TPA on the vegan Lyoner sausage

Here the oleogel moves from a standalone gel to an ingredient: it replaces the fat/oil fraction
(13% of the recipe) in a vegan Lyoner-type sausage, alongside water, soy protein isolate, citrus
fibre, carrageenan, konjac glucomannan, methylcellulose, salt, dextrose, spices, beetroot powder,
and MSG. The batter is homogenized, vacuum-packed, steam-cooked (15 min, 100°C, 100% humidity),
then ice-bathed.

Five fat sources were compared, each formed into 1.5cm-thick, 2cm-diameter rounds and tested at
4°C with a full two-cycle TPA compression (90% of sample height, 10 replicates):

- **RBX/RSO oleogel, added liquid** — mixed into the batter right after preparation, at 2/4/6/8% RBX.
- **RBX/RSO oleogel, added frozen (−20°C)** — pre-solidified, then frozen 24h before being folded
  into the batter, at 2/4/6/8% RBX.
- **RSO** — plain canola oil, no wax.
- **Margarine** and **Lard** — the conventional fat baselines.

## Results (TPA, mean ± SD of 10 replicates)

| Formulation | F1 — hardness (N) | F2 — 2nd-compression peak (N) |
|---|---|---|
| RBX/RSO 2% (liquid) | 350.1 ± 16.9 | 260.4 ± 15.3 |
| RBX/RSO 4% (liquid) | 331.1 ± 59.5 | 234.4 ± 27.5 |
| RBX/RSO 6% (liquid) | 318.3 ± 26.6 | 242.9 ± 22.1 |
| RBX/RSO 8% (liquid) | 340.5 ± 51.3 | 242.0 ± 32.1 |
| RBX/RSO 2% (−20°C) | 325.3 ± 51.1 | 237.0 ± 17.4 |
| RBX/RSO 4% (−20°C) | 342.4 ± 15.4 | 256.7 ± 16.9 |
| RBX/RSO 6% (−20°C) | 345.8 ± 14.7 | 264.3 ± 5.3 |
| RBX/RSO 8% (−20°C) | 338.9 ± 43.2 | 242.1 ± 33.2 |
| RSO (plain oil) | 335.4 ± 20.2 | 252.3 ± 83.0 |
| Margarine | 409.7 ± 31.9 | 330.6 ± 59.3 |
| Lard | 413.8 ± 25.2 | 323.3 ± 54.2 |

## What this tells us

None of the oleogel formulations reach the hardness of the margarine- or lard-based sausages
(≈410–414 N vs. ≈318–350 N) — replacing the fat with a wax-structured oil doesn't fully replicate
what a conventional solid fat does for sausage texture. But the oleogel is not equivalent to using
plain oil either: sausages made with the oleogel added **liquid** are significantly firmer than the
plain-RSO sausage, most clearly at 8% RBX, while sausages made with the oleogel added **frozen**
land closer to plain RSO. In other words, *how* the oleogel is incorporated (liquid vs. pre-frozen)
matters as much as how much wax it contains — likely because adding it liquid lets it disperse and
re-crystallize more evenly through the batter.

CLSM imaging (in the thesis, not reproduced here) showed the oleogel droplets distributed
throughout the sausage regardless of how they were added, though some oil droplets lacked visible
wax crystals — a sign of partial structural loss during homogenization or storage that isn't
quantified in this data. The thesis flags sensory testing and lipid oxidation as the natural next
steps before concluding whether this is a viable margarine/oil replacement.
