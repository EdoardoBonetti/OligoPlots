# TPA — mechanical firmness

This section covers texture testing of the **pure oleogel**, before it goes into any recipe. (For
texture testing of the finished sausage, see [TPA/SAUSAGES](SAUSAGES/index).)

**Protocol.** Oleogel (2%, 4%, 6%, 8% RBX) and lard were set directly in a plastic pot and, after
24h, penetrated with a 20mm probe at 1mm/s to a depth of 15mm (12 replicates each). Because the
sample stuck to the probe on withdrawal, only a single compression could be run — not the usual
two-cycle TPA test — so hardness (durability under a second bite) isn't measured here. What *is*
measured is:

- **F0** — the force at the first significant peak, i.e. the surface breaking under the probe.
- **F1** — the peak force reached during the compression.
- **Backward extrusion** — softer, more fluid samples get pushed sideways rather than resisting the
  probe, which shows up as the force dropping after F0 before recovering. This makes F1 hard to
  compare directly across samples with very different backward-extrusion behavior.

## Results

| | 2% RBX | 4% RBX | 6% RBX | 8% RBX | Lard |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F0 (N) | 1.74 ± 0.04 | 9.07 ± 0.19 | 8.36 ± 0.34 | 7.94 ± 0.44 | 8.22 ± 0.26 |
| F1 (N) | 1.47 ± 0.05 | 6.06 ± 0.39 | 8.12 ± 0.29 | 9.49 ± 0.25 | 8.55 ± 0.18 |

Values are mean ± SD of 12 replicates; differences are statistically significant (Tukey HSD,
p<0.05) except where samples share a group.

## What this tells us

The 2% oleogel is dramatically softer than everything else — its F0 is roughly a fifth of the
others' — while 4%, 6%, 8% RBX and lard all show broadly comparable resistance to breaking the
surface (F0). Backward extrusion becomes less pronounced as RBX% increases, meaning the gel behaves
more like a cohesive solid and less like a fluid being pushed aside as wax content rises. Overall,
firmness gains from adding more wax level off well before 8% — the biggest jump is between 2% and
4% RBX.
